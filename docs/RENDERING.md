# Rendering techniques

This document explains every rendering technique used by the game and points
to the code. The GLSL sources in `render/shaders/` carry the same explanations
as comments next to the implementation.

Frame overview (Milestones 1-2):

```
 shadow passes          scene pass (HDR, RGBA16F)               post
 ─────────────          ─────────────────────────               ────
 CSM atlas (4 tiles) ─┐
 local light atlas  ──┼─> PBR forward shader ──> scene_hdr ──> tonemap (ACES) ──> FXAA ──> screen
 sky-visibility 3D  ──┤        + sky dome           ↑             + dither
 IBL (SH + cube)    ──┘        + decals (1 draw)    │
                               + particles (2 draws)│
                               viewmodel camera ────┘ (own FOV, depth cleared, same HDR buffer)
```

## 1. Physically based materials (`pbr.frag`, `brdf.glsl`, `render/materials.py`)

* **Metal/roughness workflow.** Each material has three textures: *albedo*
  (sRGB, decoded to linear by the GPU), *normal* (tangent space, OpenGL
  convention, height in alpha) and *ORM* (R = ambient occlusion,
  G = roughness, B = metallic). Packing AO/roughness/metal into one texture
  saves samplers and bandwidth; Poly Haven ships this exact "ARM" layout.
* **BRDF.** Cook–Torrance microfacet specular: GGX normal distribution, the
  height-correlated Smith visibility term, and Schlick Fresnel, plus a
  Lambert diffuse lobe weighted by `1 - F`. Dielectrics use F0 = 0.04,
  metals use their base colour as F0 and have no diffuse.
* **Normal mapping.** Geometry carries a full tangent frame (normal, tangent,
  binormal) generated by `engine/geometry.py`. The fragment shader
  re-orthogonalises it (Gram–Schmidt) because interpolation denormalises it.
* **Parallax occlusion mapping** (High/Ultra, materials with `"parallax"`):
  the view ray is marched through the height field in tangent space
  (8–24 steps depending on view angle) and the hit is refined by linear
  interpolation between the last two samples. It fades out beyond ~16 m.
* **Specular anti-aliasing** (Tokuyoshi & Kaplanyan): roughness is widened by
  the screen-space variance of the normal so thin bright highlights on bumpy
  normal maps don't sparkle when the camera moves.
* **Micro-shadowing:** baked AO darkens direct light inside crevices too,
  which keeps mortar lines and paint chips readable in full sun.

## 2. Sun shadows: cascaded shadow maps (`render/csm.py`, `shadows.glsl`)

* The view frustum up to the shadow distance (45–120 m by preset) is split
  into 2–4 slices with the *practical split scheme* (78% logarithmic,
  22% uniform), giving more resolution close to the player.
* **Stable cascades.** Each slice is enclosed by a bounding sphere (whose
  size does not change when the camera rotates) and the cascade centre is
  snapped to whole shadow-map texels in light space. Together this removes
  the shimmering/crawling edges typical of naive CSM.
* The shadow camera is pulled back 120 m toward the sun so tall off-screen
  casters still throw shadows into view.
* All cascades share one depth atlas (2×2 tiles) rendered by one buffer with
  one display region per cascade, so the shader needs a single sampler.
* **Caster pass:** depth-only shader, colour writes off, *front faces culled*.
  Storing back-face depth moves the shadow test surface to the far side of
  closed meshes, which eliminates most self-shadowing acne.
* **Receiver bias:** normal-offset (the lookup point is pushed 1–3 texels
  along the geometric normal, more at grazing angles) plus a small constant
  depth bias proportional to texel size.
* **Cascade selection/blending:** a pixel uses the first cascade whose tile
  contains it and cross-fades to the next cascade in the outer 8% of the
  tile; shadows fade out smoothly at the shadow distance.

### Soft shadows (PCF)

The atlas is a depth texture sampled through `sampler2DShadow`, so every
tap is a hardware 2×2 bilinear depth comparison. 4–24 taps (preset) are
distributed on a **Vogel/golden-angle disk** that is rotated per pixel by
**interleaved gradient noise**; regular banding becomes fine noise that FXAA
cleans up.

## 3. Local lights and their shadows (`render/local_lights.py`, `lights.glsl`)

* Point and spot lights with physically based inverse-square falloff,
  windowed so the light reaches exactly zero at its range
  (`saturate(1-(d/r)^4)^2 / (d^2+1)`), and smooth spot cones.
* The lights nearest the camera (8–32 by preset) are packed into a small
  float texture every frame (4 texels per light), which avoids GLSL uniform
  limits and makes the light loop a few `texelFetch`es.
* **Shadow atlas:** each shadow-casting spot light owns one tile of a shared
  depth atlas; each point light owns six tiles (one per cube face, rendered
  with ~92° frusta so PCF taps near face edges stay valid). The shader picks
  the face from the dominant axis of the light→pixel vector. Per-tile
  matrices live in another float texture.
* **Update budget:** tiles are only re-rendered when needed (first frames,
  dirty lights, then a small round-robin budget per frame), so static
  lights cost almost nothing while characters walking under a lamp still
  update its shadow.
* Light fixtures get a small emissive mesh so the source is visible.

## 4. Image based lighting (`render/ibl.py`, `ibl.glsl`, `render/sky.py`)

* The sky is an **equirectangular HDR** image: a CC0 HDRI from Poly Haven,
  or a procedural sky (gradient, horizon haze, Henyey–Greenstein sun glow,
  fBm clouds) when offline.
* **Sun extraction:** the brightest region of the HDRI defines the
  shadow-casting sun direction, then the sun is clamped out of the image so
  the analytic sun light provides that energy (no double lighting, no
  fireflies). The lower hemisphere is replaced by a ground-bounce colour,
  and the sky brightness is normalised so any HDRI matches the exposure.
* **Diffuse:** the environment is projected onto 9 **spherical harmonics**
  (already convolved with the cosine lobe), evaluated per pixel with a
  handful of multiply-adds.
* **Specular:** split-sum approximation. A cube map mip chain is
  prefiltered with a GGX lobe (mip k ↔ roughness k/5) by brute-force
  convolution in numpy, then sampled with `textureLod` along the reflection
  vector. The environment BRDF term uses Karis' analytic fit instead of a
  lookup texture. Specular occlusion is derived from AO.
* Results are cached in `assets/cache/` so later launches are instant.

## 5. Sky visibility volume (`render/sky_visibility.py`)

Without global illumination, sky light would make interiors as bright as
the outdoors. At load time the level is voxelised (0.5 m) and a grid of
probes (1.5 m × 1.5 m × 1 m) casts 32 cosine-distributed rays each through
the voxels (vectorised numpy). Each probe stores the fraction of the upper
hemisphere that sees the sky and the average open direction (bent normal)
in a 3D texture. The shader samples it trilinearly, offset along the
surface normal to avoid leaking through walls, and scales the IBL terms.
Rooms are therefore lit by what comes through their doors and windows plus
their own lamps. The bake is cached per level layout.

## 6. Atmosphere (`fog.glsl`)

Analytic **exponential height fog**: density falls off with altitude and the
integral along the view ray has a closed form, so dust pools in low areas.
The fog colour comes from the sky SH (horizon average) and a
Henyey–Greenstein phase term adds forward scattering toward the sun. The
sky dome blends its horizon into the same fog colour so geometry and sky
meet without a seam.

## 7. HDR pipeline and post-processing (`render/post.py`, `tonemap.frag`, `fxaa.frag`)

* The scene renders into an **RGBA16F** target (true HDR values: the sun
  disc is ~100× brighter than white).
* **ACES filmic tone mapping** (Hill's fit of the RRT+ODT in ACEScg) with
  exposure, then the exact sRGB transfer function.
* **Dithering:** ±½ LSB triangular noise before 8-bit quantisation removes
  banding in skies and fog.
* **FXAA** (Lottes 3.11 quality-style): edge detection on luma, edge walk,
  sub-pixel blend.

Milestone 3 adds bloom, SSAO, vignette, colour grading and auto exposure on
top of this chain, plus an in-game graphics settings menu.

## 8. Procedural textures (`render/texture_gen.py`)

The offline fallback synthesises tileable PBR sets: spectral fBm
(random-phase noise shaped by 1/f^β and inverse-FFT'd, so it tiles
perfectly), wrapped Worley noise for pores/cracks/pebbles, and analytic
patterns (bricks, planks, tiles, corrugation, diamond plate) perturbed by
noise. Normals come from height via central differences; AO from a cavity
term (blurred height minus height). Grime on metals is modelled as a
dielectric dust layer (lower metalness, higher roughness).

## 9. Viewmodel (`weapons/viewmodel.py`, `weapons/models.py`, `data/weapon_models.json`)

* **Separate camera, same lighting.** The arms and gun hang under the main
  camera in the scene graph, so they get exactly the sun, IBL, local lights
  and shadows at the player's position. They are hidden from the world and
  shadow cameras through camera masks. A second camera with its own lens
  (viewmodel FOV, default 54° vertical) draws them into the same HDR buffer
  after the world, through a display region that clears depth first. The gun
  therefore never clips into walls and keeps its proportions when the player
  changes FOV or zooms.
* **Procedural, data-driven models.** Each weapon is a list of chamfered
  boxes, cylinders and spheres in `data/weapon_models.json` with PBR
  materials (gun metal, polymer, wood, brass, gloves, sleeves). Named groups
  (magazine, bolt, slide, pump) can move on their own, and anchors mark the
  muzzle, ejection port, sight and hand grips. Arms are two segments solved
  from the grip anchors and a per-weapon elbow hint. Real meshes can replace
  them later without touching gameplay code.
* **Animation layers** (all procedural, summed every frame):
  * hip/ADS pose: ADS places the sight anchor on the screen centre
  * draw
  * mouse sway: a critically damped spring
  * idle breathing
  * a figure-eight walk bob tied to the footstep phase
  * crouch and landing offsets
  * a per-shot kick spring
  * keyframe tracks (`weapons/anim_data.py`): reload (partly full and empty),
    bolt and pump cycles, shell-by-shell loading, inspect, knife and grenade
    throws. Tracks use smoothstep interpolation, and the left hand follows
    the magazine.
* **Camera punch vs. aim.** Recoil moves the true aim point along the
  weapon's fixed pattern. The camera follows only part of it
  (`view_follow`), and a separate visual punch spring adds a short kick.
  That kick never changes where bullets land.

## 10. Particles (`render/particles.py`, `particle.vert`, `particle.frag`)

* Two systems: alpha-blended (smoke, dust, debris) and additive (sparks,
  fire, flashes, tracers). Each draws in **one call**: the live particles
  are simulated with numpy (structure of arrays) and written into one dynamic
  vertex buffer, 4 vertices per particle.
* The **vertex shader** expands each particle into a quad in world space. A
  normal particle is a rotated, camera-facing billboard. A *stretched*
  particle (sparks, tracers) has its long axis along the screen-projected
  velocity and is lengthened by its speed, which acts as a cheap motion blur.
* Textures come from a procedural 4×4 atlas: soft puffs, three smoke shapes,
  spark, glow, star, side-on muzzle flash, chunk, splinter, mist, fire, ring,
  dust, streak and shard.
* Alpha particles are **lit**: sky SH irradiance plus a share of the sun,
  scaled by the baked sky-visibility volume, so smoke indoors is darker than
  smoke outside. Additive particles output HDR values (muzzle flashes reach
  about 8× white), so they will drive bloom in Milestone 3.
* Alpha particles are depth-sorted back to front every frame. Soft fading
  with age and size curves keeps them from popping.

## 11. Decals (`render/decals.py`, `pbr.frag` with `DECAL`)

* All bullet holes, blast scorches and blood splats share **one draw call**:
  one dynamic vertex buffer used as a ring (640 quads by default). The
  oldest decal is overwritten when the buffer is full, and only the 4
  changed vertices are uploaded (`setSubdata`).
* Decals use the **same PBR shader** compiled with `DECAL`, with procedural
  albedo/alpha, normal and ORM atlases. Holes are therefore lit, shadowed,
  fogged and reflect the IBL exactly like the surface they sit on:
  * concrete and brick: a dark core, a chipped crater with normal-mapped
    walls and a pale dust halo
  * metal: a bright scraped ring, low roughness and high metalness
  * wood: splintered fibres
  * glass: a radial crack star
* Each quad is aligned to the hit normal with a random roll and lifted 4 mm
  to avoid z-fighting. Size and style come from the surface entry in
  `data/surfaces.json`.

## 12. Gameplay effects (`render/effects.py`)

* **Impacts:** each surface type has its own recipe for dust colour, debris
  (chips, splinters, sparks), a decal style and an impact sound. Metal
  throws stretched sparks with gravity, wood throws splinters, and flesh
  (the dummies) gets a mist of blood plus a splat decal on the wall behind.
* **Muzzle flash:** three additive sprites (a star, two stretched side
  flames and a glow) drawn at the viewmodel's muzzle anchor in viewmodel
  space, so they line up with the barrel at any FOV. There is also a wisp of
  lit world-space smoke. A 60 ms **point light pulse** goes into the local
  light list, so nearby walls, the floor and the gun itself light up for a
  few frames.
* **Tracers:** stretched additive particles that fly at 420 m/s from the
  muzzle to the impact point. Each weapon sets its own tracer cadence.
* **Shell casings:** small instanced meshes with ballistic motion, spin and
  a floor bounce found with one ray cast when they spawn. They are kept out
  of the shadow pass, and at most 28 exist at once.
* **Explosions:** a fireball (additive), a dark smoke column (alpha),
  radial debris and sparks, a scorch decal, a 0.4 s light pulse of 260
  intensity units, and camera shake that falls off with distance.
* **Smoke grenade:** about 70 large lit smoke particles grow outward to the
  cloud radius over 2 s, live for the smoke's duration (18 s) and fade out.
  `SmokeCloud.density` and `Effects.smoke_between(a, b)` let gameplay test
  line of sight through smoke (bots use it in Milestone 5).
* **Flashbang:** a very bright light pulse and a glow sprite. Gameplay
  blinds the player with a full-screen white card whose duration depends on
  distance and viewing angle.

## Performance notes

* Level geometry is batched per material: about 26 draw calls for the
  test range's main pass, plus 1 for all decals and 2 for all particles.
* The viewmodel has about 20-60 small parts per weapon. Only the equipped
  weapon is shown, and models are built once and cached, so switching
  weapons costs nothing.
* Per-frame Python work (camera, cascades, light packing) is ~1 ms; all
  heavy lifting is on the GPU or precomputed and cached.
* Quality knobs per preset (`data/graphics_presets.json`): cascade count and
  resolution, shadow distance, PCF taps, local shadow atlas/tile/budget,
  light count, parallax, specular AA, texture size and anisotropy.
